# Minimal neon reservoir: evidence for model planning

Status: research note, 2026-10-30. Scope: published experimental mechanisms and ngspice capabilities relevant to the approved simulation design. No new simulation or physical measurement has been performed. Refer also to `ne2_circuit_literature.md` for the wider source inventory.

## Evidence and transfer limits

- **Cited — NE-2AS reference, not a measurement of our lamp:** The [Glow Lamp Specifications catalogue](https://www.effectrode.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/glow_lamp_specifications.pdf) lists initial DC breakdown as 60/76/90 V minimum/average/maximum at 5–50 footcandles, average maintaining voltage 58 V at 0.3 mA, design current 0.3 mA, and minimum extinction voltage 50 V for a test with at least 0.25 MΩ series resistance. The catalogue's 50 V is not a nominal extinction value. The available scan does not clearly identify its manufacturer. The part actually used must be identified; these values do not establish the behavior of another NE-2 variant.
- **Cited — another product, different information:** [Premier Farnell, Neon Glow Lamps](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/1657692.pdf), page 1, lists A1A (NE-2) with 0.6 mA current, 90 V maximum DC breakdown, and 100 kΩ series resistor for the listed 100–125 V circuit range. These are product-specific entries, not a proposal for a safe experimental operating point; note the distinction between circuit voltage and bulb voltage. The sheet does not specify bulb holding-current distribution or dynamics.
- **Cited — experimental neon ignition delay:** Marković, Stamenković, Gocić and Durić, “Determination and modelling of the formative and statistical time delay in neon,” *European Physical Journal Applied Physics* 38, 73–78 (2007), [publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1051/epjap:2007051), report a dependence of formative delay on working voltage, discuss separate statistical and formative distributions, and introduce time-dependent statistical distributions in sputtering conditions. The abstract does not establish a fixed Gaussian formative distribution, a constant hazard, or NE-2-specific parameter values. Access to complete experimental tables and fitted equations remains necessary before numerical reuse.
- **Cited — afterglow memory example:** [Marković et al., published conference paper](https://publications.aob.rs/84/pdf/289-292.pdf), pp. 289–292, present the breakdown delay as a function of afterglow time in a neon tube, with experimental conditions including 271 V static breakdown, 320 V working voltage, 0.1 mA glow current and 1 s glow duration. Its reported setup is not an NE-2 validation. The paper discusses both afterglow ion kinetics and cathode-related processes; it cannot uniquely identify memory mechanisms in our bulb.
- **Cited — simplified neon bulb model:** Kennedy and Chua, “Van der Pol and Chaos,” *IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems* 33, 974–980 (1986), [author institutional record and abstract](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/17728/), report reproducing period adding in a driven neon relaxation circuit using a three-segment piecewise-linear current-controlled resistor. This motivates a candidate electrical model, but neither proves NE-2 active-glow memory nor supplies its parameters. The full paper was not inspected for numerical values here.
- **Cited — continuous discharge model:** Pugliese et al., “Complex dynamics of a dc glow discharge tube: Experimental modeling and stability diagrams,” *Scientific Reports* 5, 8447 (2015), [publisher record/abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep08447), describe an experimentally informed macroscopic model with four autonomous ODEs for a discharge tube. This is evidence that models with added states can describe measured discharge-system dynamics, not evidence that those four states or its coefficients apply to the NE-2. The full publisher page was inaccessible during this check; no specific internal-state equation or numeric parameter is adopted from it.

**Derived:** One current/voltage operating point cannot determine the slope, nonlinear shape, time constant, hysteresis, or optical response of the conducting branch. No value should be inferred uniquely from the catalogue average. Published minima and maxima are not a probability distribution and do not establish parameter correlations.

**Unknown:** Our device's exact subtype and illumination; its static and dynamic I–V curve, current holding/extinction behavior, ignition hazard versus overvoltage and afterglow, formative-delay statistics, glow relaxation, optical transfer function, and internal capacitance. Any proposed NE-2 range for those quantities is exploratory unless a directly applicable source or measurement establishes it. A comparison of alternative models is sensitivity analysis, not a validation ranking.

## ngspice capability and verification implications

- **Cited:** ngspice documents [behavioral sources controlled by time-varying random sources](https://nmg.gitlab.io/ngspice-manual/statisticalcircuitanalysis/behavioralsources_b,e,g,r,l,c_withrandomcontrol.html). A random waveform driving a behavioral source is not by itself a model of history- and voltage-dependent stochastic ignition; its event process and physical interpretation must be constructed and verified.
- **Cited:** The [ngspice transient-noise manual](https://nmg.gitlab.io/ngspice-manual/analysesandoutputcontrol_batchmode/analyses/transientnoiseanalysis_atlowfrequency.html) describes selecting a seed for repeatable noise and explicitly calls transient noise experimental, listing unresolved calibration and timestep issues. It describes white, 1/f and random-telegraph noise; these are not automatically equivalent to discharge initiation. Record ngspice version, seed method, transient noise interval, integration method, tolerances and maximum timestep, then test reproducibility and timestep convergence for the actual installed version.
- **Inferred:** If an externally generated event mechanism is selected, a precomputed event schedule is only correct for the model it represents. State-dependent rates require verified interaction with the actual evolving node voltage and history; externally injecting random fixed events without that coupling cannot support the intended claim. Document how solver rollback and event ordering affect reproducibility.
- **Inferred:** Comparisons should independently vary internal memory assumptions, ballast, parasitic capacitances and measured/simulated sensor response. A change in computed performance demonstrates sensitivity to the model assumptions; it does not establish that the physical bulb has those dynamics. Calibrate and validate pulse, I–V, rest-time and continuously lit response against separate measurements before claiming accuracy.
